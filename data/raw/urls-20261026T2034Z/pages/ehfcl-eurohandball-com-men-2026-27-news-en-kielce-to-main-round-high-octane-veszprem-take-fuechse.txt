Kielce to main round; high-octane Veszprém take Füchse
Kielce to main round; high-octane Veszprém take Füchse
EHF / Kevin Domas
21 October 2026, 23:00
Share URL via E-Mail
Share on Facebook
Share on Twitter
Share on WhatsApp
Copy URL to clipboard
The group phase of the EHF Champions League Men 2026/27 is heading towards its final stages, with only three game days left after the opening of round 5 on Wednesday night. And while some fates were decided, some tickets for the main round are still up for grabs.
The only team that qualified on Wednesday was Industria Kielce, who had to battle for 60 minutes to overcome HC Kriens-Luzern’s resistance. SAH - Aarhus missed the opportunity to book their main round ticket, losing badly against Dinamo in Bucharest.
In group A, One Veszprém HC made sure they would take the maximum of points with them into the main round, while RK Partizan AdmiralBet secured their first point in the EHF Champions League Men 2026/27.
Everything is still open in a couple of groups, such as group B, where HBC Nantes put themselves in a precarious position by losing against Vardar. After their results on Wednesday, a win and a loss, respectively, Aalborg Håndbold and Paris Saint-Germain will battle for the top spot in group C next week.
Veszprém will take four points with them into the main round after their second win of the season against Berlin, 39:35
despite only needing a point in round 5, Aarhus did not secure their presence in the main round as they suffered a tough defeat against Dinamo, 37:23
already qualified, Paris were defeated in Zagreb, 29:28, while Aalborg are sole leaders in group C after their victory over Celje, 34:30
Vardar grabbed their first victory of the season in Nantes, 33:27, while Partizan took their first point, drawing against Porto 33:33
Kielce had to wait until the final minutes of their game in Kriens-Luzern, when they clinched a 32:30 result, to become the eighth team qualified for the main round
GROUP A
One Veszprém HC (HUN) vs Füchse Berlin (GER) 39:35 (20:13)
H2H: 5-0-5
Top scorers: Dragan Pechmalbec 8/9 (One Veszprém HC); Mathias Gidsel 9/12 (Füchse Berlin)
One could be sure that Berlin would have some nightmares featuring Emil Nielsen on Wednesday night, after the Danish goalkeeper gave another crazy performance for Veszprém. The hosts did not take a goal for the first five minutes of the game, as Nielsen made seven saves in the opening quarter. As a consequence, his team was already ahead by four after 10 minutes. The situation did not get any better for the visitors when Matthes Langhoff was sent off after hitting Ali Zein in the face in minute 15. As they made the most of every opportunity, Veszprém’s lead increased to nine goals shortly before the break; an advantage that two goals from Berlin’s Mijajlo Marsenić reduced to seven ahead of half-time. Things could not get any worse for Füchse and, luckily for them, they did not. Finally finding some offensive solutions in line players Max Darj and Marsenić, they scored three in a row to come back within three with 15 minutes left to play. Veszprém held tight though, and Nedim Remili proved important in the last 12 minutes, scoring three and delivering two assists. Berlin were never able to make things level and Veszprém enjoyed their fifth victory in as many games this season.

The game wasn’t easy. The Füchse players showed once again that they are true to themselves — they always approach the game with great focus. Berlin played brilliantly, but our performance in the first half was simply outstanding. However, at this level, it’s very difficult to maintain that standard throughout an entire match. Berlin scored more goals in the second half and switched to a seven-against-six attack, which made things more difficult for us defensively as well. But the amount of work we put in during the first half was incredible.
Xavi Pascual
Head coach, One Veszprém HC
It was clear that the opening stages proved decisive. Our decision-making at the start, particularly our shot selection against Emil Nielsen, was not good enough, and that shaped the rest of the match. However, I think we did a much better job in the second half, with Mathias helping us stick to our game plan and try to find solutions. Ultimately, we had no answer to Veszprém in the opening stages, and the advantage they built was enough to see them through to the final whistle.
Nicolej Krickau
Head coach, Füchse Berlin

Highlight Match: RK Partizan AdmiralBet (SRB) vs FC Porto (POR) 33:33 (14:16)
H2H: 2-1-1
Top scorers: Nikola Crnoglavac 8/10 (RK Partizan AdmiralBet); Pedro Oliveira 6/7 (FC Porto)
Despite both teams being eliminated, Partizan and Porto knew the importance of Wednesday’s game: Two points to take into the EHF European League for the winner. Right from the off, Porto seemed quicker to settle into their rhythm. As Sebastian Abrahamsson pulled off a couple of important saves and Ricardo Brandão’s hands did not shake when it came to scoring, the Portuguese side took an early three-goal advantage. The visitors remained on top until the break, despite Yoav Lumbroso and Tobias Wagner doing their best to keep their side in the race. What Porto did not manage to do before the break, they did after it. As Abrahamsson saved three shots in a row, the Portuguese side scored as many straight goals and soon broke away to sit seven ahead. That was not the end of Belgrade’s hopes though: In a desperate run of honour, Nikola Crnoglavac scored three in a row. As a result, the visitors came back within one, and even offered themselves the opportunity to draw on the last offence. Lazar Andjelkovic did not miss the opportunity, offering his club its first point in the competition.

GROUP B
HBC Nantes (FRA) vs HC Vardar 1961 (MKD) 27:33 (13:16)
H2H: 2-0-2
Top scorers: Ihor Turchenko 4/6, Luka Peric 4/6 (Nantes); Leon Ljevar 7/9 (HC Vardar 1961)
The first half of Wednesday’s confrontation was all about goalkeepers. Vardar’s Miłosz Wałach first put himself into the spotlight, helping the visitors take a four-goal lead with six saves within the opening quarter. “Le H” used the turning point of a missed fast-break chance for Vardar to steal the momentum, thanks to Christoffer Bonde’s saves, but the French side never managed to level the game after the first minutes. Worse, they could not prevent Vardar breaking away again and with Uroš Mitrović driving with four goals in the first half, the visitors finished ahead by three at the break. Vardar kept the momentum in the second half, as Wałach was still on fire between the posts. But this time, his teammates were able to knock Nantes on the head, with Jaka Malus and Leon Ljevar giving the North Macedonian side an eight-goal advantage six minutes before the siren. Junior Matiaba-Tuzolana pulled the advantage across the 10-goal mark at the 56th minute, securing Vardar’s first win of the season.

GROUP C
HC Zagreb (CRO) vs Paris Saint-Germain (FRA) 29:28 (13:17)
H2H: 3-0-14
Top scorers: Ihar Bialiauski 8/10 (HC Zagreb); Ferran Solé 10/11 (Paris Saint-Germain)
Despite being eliminated from the main round race, Zagreb had no intention of playing a friendly game against Paris on Wednesday night. With Giorgi Tskhovrebadze scoring three in the first 10 minutes, the hosts kept up with Paris’ tempo early on. But as Ferran Solé did not miss anything and passed Zagreb’s keepers six times in the first half alone, the French side slowly but surely created a clear upper hand as the half-time break approached. The second half was completely different, as Zagreb took the momentum right from the start. With Sandro Meštric working up to nine saves and Ihar Bialiauski on scoring duties, the hosts came back little by little. Filip Glavaš levelled the score with five minutes to play, though Zagreb’s defence held tight. Recovering a couple of Paris’ turnovers, the Croatian side moved ahead for the first time in the second half within the last five minutes, before PSG wasted their last opportunity to equalise in the final seconds. Thanks to this win, Zagreb secured third spot in the group.

Aalborg Håndbold (DEN) vs RK Celje Pivovarna Laško (SLO) 34:30 (17:13)
H2H: 8-0-1
Top scorers: Thomas Arnoldsen 6/8 (Aalborg Håndbold); Tadej Mazej 5/5, Aljuš Anžic 5/10, Mattis Heiss 5/5 (RK Celje Pivovarna Laško)
Aalborg coach Simon Dahl had decided to use the game against Celje to offer playing time to lesser-used players, and the first half validated his decision. With Norwegian right back Patrick Anderson a key force in attack, the Danish side broke away early — already ahead by seven after 19 minutes. The Slovenian side stabilised their deficit to four goals before the break, thanks to Gal Gaberšek making a couple of saves. Aalborg remained in control in the second half, unable to increase their advantage further than six goals, but not letting their opponents come too close either. Juri Knorr made an impression coming on the court, scoring three within 10 minutes after the break — enough for Aalborg to secure the victory, which was their fourth across the first five games of their European season.

GROUP E
Dinamo Bucuresti (ROU) vs SAH - Aarhus (DEN) 37:23 (16:12)
H2H: 1-1-0
Top scorers: Victor Iturizza 5/5 (Dinamo Bucuresti); Andreas Søgaard Rasmussen 4/4, Jeppe Månsson Cieslak 4/6 (SAH - Aarhus)
Was it the pressure to give themselves the chance of grabbing a ticket to the main round? No matter the reason, Aarhus did not have the best start to Wednesday’s game. Struggling offensively and reluctant to use their seven-against-six tactics in the first half, the Danish side were clearly lacking some offensive strength. Dinamo, on the other hand, were quick to find solutions, with Victor Iturizza and Haniel Langaro in the spotlight. As the two scored three goals in the first 21 minutes, the hosts took the maximum advantage of four goals in the first period — a lead they retained at the break. When Dinamo returned to court, they made the most of their opponents’ errors to increase their lead to eight seven minutes after the restart. That was the end of it for the Aarhus, who were never able to come back. On the other hand, Dinamo coach Paulo Perreira used the opportunity to open his bench and rotate most of his players on the court, with only one field player not scoring as their team enjoyed their first win of the season — and their biggest ever in the EHF Champions League.

GROUP F
HC Kriens-Luzern (SUI) vs Industria Kielce (POL) 30:32 (17:19)
H2H: 0-0-2
Top scorers: Marin Šipić 8/10 (HC Kriens-Luzern); Théo Monar 8/8 (Industria Kielce)
Kielce’s players knew well that they could book their main round ticket by winning in Switzerland — and their French players seemed highly motivated by this prospect, judging from their first-half performances. Théo Monar and Julien Bos scored a combined 11 goals before the break. But the Polish team were never able to take an advantage bigger than four goals, as their hosts opened strong and kept it tight. As Marin Šipić and Jerome Müller spearheaded the attack, Kriens-Luzern had everything open at half-time. The second period was a long neck-to-neck battle, with neither team able to take an advantage bigger than two goals. Despite Müller receiving a red card at minute 39, Kriens-Luzern kept up with the opponents’ rhythm throughout. Things came down to the last three minutes. Piotr Jarosiewicz scored two before Aleks Vlah netted the game winner, while the Swiss team failed to convert their last two offences and buried their main round hopes.
