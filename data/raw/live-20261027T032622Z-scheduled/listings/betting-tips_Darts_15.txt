Best Free Darts Selections
Our darts tipsters share predictions across a wide range of betting markets. Here is a guide to the main markets you will see next to the darts tips:
Betting Market
What It Means
Tournament Winner
The selection receiving the most predictions to win the tournament outright, displayed under the tournament name, for example, the PDC World Championship or the PDC Premier League. Tournament Winner picks may also include 'Each Way' selections, particularly for events like the PDC World Championship with a large pool of players. Each Way terms in most tournaments cover 1st and 2nd place, so the player must reach the final to at least return a partial payout. Clicking the pick takes you to the tournament tips page showing all the predictions, including the 'Each Way' picks.
Win Match
A straight-win bet. The selection shown for each match has received the most predictions and is tipped to win the match.
Handicap 2 Way
A handicap bet where bookies set the Handicap Line. Most regular PDC tournaments are in 'leg' format, so the handicap may be set at + or - 1.5 legs. The favourite starts at -1.5 legs and the underdog at +1.5 legs. If you think the favourite will win by more than 1 leg, the bet is -1.5 legs; if you feel the match will be decided within 1 leg or the underdog wins, the bet is +1.5 legs.
Total Legs (or Total Sets)
A line bet where bookies set their forecast total number of legs or sets to be played. Most tournaments use a shorter format, so this is a Total Legs bet. In a best-of-11-legs match, the first player to 6 legs wins, and bookies may set the line at Over or Under 9.5 legs: Over if you expect the match to go the distance to an 11th and deciding leg, Under if you expect it settled in the 9th leg or earlier. For 'Set' format tournaments such as the PDC World Championship, the Total Sets market applies instead, with the line set at the total number of sets played.
Win First Set
Applicable only to 'Set' format tournaments like the PDC World Championship. You are betting on which player wins the First Set; the final match result is irrelevant.
Leg (or Set) Betting
A correct score bet on the final leg or set score in the match. For most regular tournaments, bookies offer Leg Betting and, as with a correct score in football, a correct Leg Score or Set Score bet offers greater payouts than the win market alone. For 'Set' format events like the PDC World Championship, bookies offer set-score betting.
Total 180s
A line bet on the combined number of 180s thrown by both players, where you predict 'Over' or 'Under' the figure set by the bookies. Consideration should be given to the likely number of legs or sets in the match and whether the players are generally prolific with maximums. Some players are more likely to drop to the 19s if the first dart of a visit lands awkwardly and blocks a clean shot at the treble 20 — a factor OLBG tipsters may weigh before adding darts tips.
Most 180s
A match bet on which player throws the most 180s. It is usually a 3-way market that includes the tie option.
9 Dart Finish
A straight 'Yes' or 'No' bet on whether a player will throw a perfect 9-dart leg. The '9 darter' happens occasionally in major televised events, so it can be a fun wager on the 'Yes' option, offering a good return for a small outlay.

Darts Betting Strategy: What to Look For
Darts is one of the most statistics-friendly sports to bet on, because the numbers that matter are published and consistently tracked. If you are new to darts betting, a handful of key indicators will help you read a match before backing a selection.
Three-Dart Average
A player's three-dart average is the most cited measure of form — the total points scored divided by darts thrown, multiplied by three. A higher average generally points to stronger scoring, but averages are harder to sustain over longer matches, so weigh a player's average against the format and the strength of the opponent.
Checkout Percentage
Scoring heavily means little if a player cannot finish. Checkout percentage measures how often a player converts their attempts at a double to close out a leg. A big scorer with a poor checkout rate can be vulnerable, while a clinical finisher can punish any missed doubles — a key edge to look for in tighter matches.
180s and Scoring Power
Some players are far more prolific at hitting maximums than others. A player's 180 rate is worth checking for the Total 180s and Most 180s markets, and it also feeds into scoring power over a leg. Consider the likely number of legs or sets too, as a longer match offers more opportunities for maximums.
Head-to-Head and Recent Form
Past meetings between two players can reveal a pattern, but head-to-head records should never be read alone — players improve, confidence shifts, and recent form often tells you more than results from months ago. Look at how a player has performed in their last few matches, and on the same stage where possible, as floor form and big-stage TV form can differ.
Format and the Draw
The format shapes how a match plays out. Shorter leg-format matches can throw up surprises, while longer set-format events such as the PDC World Championship tend to reward consistency and favour the stronger player over time. Factor the format into every darts bet, particularly for handicap and total legs or sets markets.
Darts Betting Terms Explained
Darts has plenty of its own language. Here are some of the key terms you will come across when reading darts tips and following a match:
Term
Meaning
Leg
A single game, usually from 501 down to zero. The first player to reach zero with a double wins the leg.
Set
A group of legs. In set-format events, a player must win a set number of legs (often best of five) to take the set. Used in tournaments like the PDC World Championship.
180
The maximum score with three darts, hitting three treble 20s. Also called a maximum.
Ton
A score of 100 or more with three darts. A "ton-plus" checkout is a finish of 100 or more.
Checkout
The finishing score that takes a player from their remaining total to exactly zero, ending on a double. The highest possible three-dart checkout is 170.
Double
The outer ring of the board, scoring twice the segment value. A leg can only be won by landing the final dart on a double (or the bullseye).
Bogey Number
A remaining score that cannot be checked out in three darts: 169, 168, 166, 165, 163, 162 and 159. Players aim to avoid leaving these.
Nine-Dart Finish
A perfect leg — checking out 501 in the minimum nine darts. A rare feat, sometimes backed in the "9 Dart Finish" market.
Three-Dart Average
The average score per three darts across a match — the most common measure of a player's scoring form.
Throw / Visit
One turn of three darts. The player who throws first in a leg is said to "have the darts", which is a slight advantage.

What is the best darts bet for beginners?
If you are new to darts betting, the Win Match market (simply backing a player to win) and the Tournament Winner market are the most straightforward places to start. As you get more comfortable, markets like Handicap, Total Legs and Most 180s offer more variety. Our darts betting strategy section above explains the key stats worth checking before placing any bet.
What does each-way mean in darts betting?
An each-way bet is two bets in one: half your stake on a player to win the tournament, and half on them to "place". In most darts tournaments, the place terms cover the top two, so the player needs to reach the final for the place part to pay out. Each-way betting is most common in outright tournament markets with a large field, such as the PDC World Championship.
When is the next major darts tournament?
The darts calendar runs almost all year round, so there is usually an event on the horizon. Check the tips feed and the TV Darts Schedule near the top of this page to see the next major tournament our tipsters are covering, along with their latest darts predictions for it.
Which is the biggest darts tournament?
The PDC World Darts Championship, held at Alexandra Palace over the Christmas and New Year period, is widely regarded as the sport's biggest event. It carries the largest prize fund and the most prestige, and it attracts the biggest field, which is why it is one of the most popular tournaments for darts betting each year.

Yes. Premier League Darts is one of our most popular events for darts tips. The competition runs weekly from February through to the play-offs in late May, and our tipsters add their predictions for each night throughout the season, covering match winners and a range of other betting markets.
