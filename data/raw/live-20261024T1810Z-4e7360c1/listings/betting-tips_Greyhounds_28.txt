How greyhound races are graded
Graded races make up most of the daily cards. Each race carries a letter for its distance category and a number for its class, with 1 the highest grade (so A1 is stronger than A5). The most common prefixes you'll see on a racecard are:
Prefix
Race type
A
Standard-distance graded race
D
Sprint
S
Stayers
E
Marathon
P
Puppy race
OR
Open race, where dogs are selected on merit rather than by grade
Traps, reserves and jacket colours
British greyhound races have a maximum of six runners, one from each trap. If a dog is withdrawn through injury or illness, a reserve can take its place when a suitable one is available. Reserves are marked with an ‘R’ on the racecard and on their racing jacket.
Each trap has its own jacket colour:
Trap 1: Red
Trap 2: Blue
Trap 3: White
Trap 4: Black
Trap 5: Orange
Trap 6: Black and white stripes

TV Trophy
The TV Trophy is the leading marathon event of the year. It moves between tracks and is run over the host track's marathon trip, with recent renewals staged at Oxford and Towcester. It takes its name from the BBC, which televised it from 1958 until 1996. The most celebrated winner is Scurlogue Champ, who won in 1985 and 1986. He was famous for starting slowly and then cutting through the field with a devastating finish, which made him a huge crowd favourite. Among current trainers, Mark Wallis and Paul Young have each won it five times.

What trap number wins most often in greyhound racing?
With six runners, each trap would win around 16.7% of the time if the draw made no difference. In practice, the figures vary from track to track depending on the shape of the circuit and the run to the first bend. A trap that wins well above that baseline at a particular track may point to a draw bias worth factoring into your selections.

FAQ
Greyhound Racing Tips FAQs
Are OLBG's greyhound racing tips free?
Yes. Every greyhound tip on this page is free. The tips are posted by OLBG members, and each race shows the most-tipped dog along with how many tips it has received and the comments behind the picks.
How do you pick a winner in a greyhound race?
Start with the tips on this page, which show the most-tipped dog in each race along with the reasoning behind the picks, and compare them with your own view of the form.
Early pace is one of the most important factors. Races are often decided by the run to the first bend, where crowding and bumping are common, so a dog that is quick out of the traps and likely to lead avoids most of the trouble. Check each dog's recent sectional (first-bend) times as well as its overall run times.
Also consider whether a dog's running style suits its trap. A dog that likes to run close to the rail is better drawn inside than out wide. Look for dogs moving down in grade, or young, improving dogs that could still be ahead of their mark.
What trap number wins the most in greyhound racing?
No single trap wins most often at every track. In 2025, Trap 1 was the best-performing trap at six tracks and Trap 6 at five, while Traps 2, 3 and 4 each came out on top elsewhere.
With six runners, each trap would win around 16.7% of the time if the draw made no difference. The biggest biases in 2025 were Trap 6 at Harlow and Trap 1 at Towcester, both winning more than 23% of the time. Bias depends mainly on each track's layout and the run to the first bend, so check the track-by-track trap figures on this page before betting.
Does the favourite have a good record in greyhound racing?
Yes. In 2025, the outright favourite won 35.8% of graded races across the tracks in our table, roughly one race in three. That varied from 31.1% at Kinsley to 42.5% at Valley.
Favourites win more often in open races, where the gap in class between runners tends to be wider. In 2025, open-race favourites won 48.0% of the time overall, from 54.1% at Oxford down to 41.8% at Romford. You'll find the full track-by-track figures further up this page.
Can I watch greyhound racing on TV?
Yes. Sky Sports Racing shows selected greyhound meetings, mainly evening open racing and the big events. Almost every race at GBGB tracks is also streamed live through bookmaker websites and apps, where you'll usually need a funded account or a qualifying bet to watch.
What type of greyhound bets do OLBG tips cover?
OLBG's greyhound tips are win tips, so every selection on this page is a pick to win the race. The win market is the simplest greyhound bet: if your dog finishes first, your bet wins.
Bookmakers also offer bets such as forecasts (first and second in the correct order) and tricasts (first, second and third in the correct order). Our [href=/bookmakers/articles/greyhounds-betting-advice] greyhound betting guide explains these and other bet types in more detail.
How do handicaps work in greyhound racing?
In a handicap race, dogs of differing ability start from staggered positions to even out their chances. The fastest dog starts furthest back, known as running off "scratch", while slower dogs are given a head start. Each dog's starting allowance is shown on the racecard. Handicaps are much less common than graded races at British tracks.
