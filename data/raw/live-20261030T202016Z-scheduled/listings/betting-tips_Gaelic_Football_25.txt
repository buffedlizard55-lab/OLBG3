Gaelic Football Competitions to Bet On
Gaelic Football tips cover a wide range of competitions across the season, from the national leagues right through to the club game. Here are the main ones you can expect selections for:
National Football League: The league is the main early-season competition, running through the spring. It is split into four divisions, with Division 1 featuring the top counties and Divisions 2 to 4 below it. Teams are promoted and relegated between divisions each year, and the divisional structure means there is competitive football and plenty of tipping opportunities across all four tiers.
All-Ireland Senior Football Championship (Sam Maguire): The showpiece of the Gaelic Football calendar. The winners lift the Sam Maguire Cup, and this is the competition every county wants to win. It runs through the summer and culminates in the All-Ireland final at Croke Park.
Tailteann Cup: The secondary inter-county championship, contested by teams outside the top tier of the Sam Maguire competition. It has quickly become an important part of the season, giving more counties a realistic shot at silverware and adding depth to the summer's tipping.
Provincial Championships: Before the All-Ireland series, counties compete in their provincial championships — Munster, Leinster, Ulster and Connacht. These are steeped in tradition and local rivalry, and they feed into the wider All-Ireland picture.
Sigerson Cup: The leading third-level (university and college) competition, often a first look at the emerging talent who go on to star at inter-county level.
Club Football: Club championships run at county, provincial and All-Ireland level, with the season traditionally reaching its climax on St Patrick's Day. Club football offers a huge volume of matches to get involved in.
Ladies' Gaelic Football: We also cover the Ladies' game, including the league and the All-Ireland Ladies' Senior Football Championship.
All-Ireland Football Championship Winners
The main tournament is the [href=/ie/blogs/all-ireland-football-betting-preview-betting-tips-guide] All-Ireland Football Championship, established in 1887. This is a long-standing tournament, which is an inter-county competition.
Kerry hold the all-time record with 39 titles, ahead of Dublin on 31 — the only two counties to have reached double figures.
The reigning champions are Mayo, who ended a 75-year wait in 2026, defeating Kerry in the final to claim their fourth title.

Gaelic Football betting markets explained
The Full-Time Result match tips will always be available, and there may be tips for other markets like Total Points and 3-Way Handicap, as explained below:
Full-Time Result: This is as straightforward as it sounds; you are backing either the Home team to win, the Away team to win, or the game to end in a draw.
Total Points: This is the total number of points scored in the match, and you are backing either over or under the set line.
There are different types of scores in GAA sports. A goal is worth 3 points, a point scored from inside the 40-metre arc is worth 1 point, and a point kicked from outside the arc is worth 2 points.
Because of this, a scoreline is now usually shown with a combined total in brackets. For example, a score of 4-18 (30) means 4 goals (12 points) and 18 points from a mix of one-point and two-point scores, adding up to 30 in total.
The bracketed figure is the one to look at for the overall total, as the middle number alone no longer tells you how many two-pointers were scored.
3-Way Handicap: The 3-way handicap is not too different from the Asian Handicap. The only difference is that the draw is included, making it a 3-way.
Let's say Clare is playing Cork; the handicap is set as follows: Clare (-1) and Cork (+1). Meaning if you backed Clare, they would need to win by at least 2 points.
If you back the draw, to be successful, the team with the negative handicap needs to win by that exact number. Meaning Clare would need to win by 1 point for the draw to be a winner.
For Cork (+1) to be successful, all they have to do is not lose the game because they have a 1-point head-start.
New Gaelic Football Rules and What They Mean for Betting
Gaelic Football looks different to how it did a couple of seasons ago. The Football Review Committee (FRC), chaired by Jim Gavin, introduced a package of rule changes that were trialled throughout 2025. The GAA's Special Congress passed all 62 of the FRC's recommended rule changes, and the changes came into effect on 1 January 2026, so they are now a permanent part of the game rather than a temporary experiment.
For anyone betting on Gaelic Football, the most important change is how scoring works, as it has a direct effect on Total Points and handicap markets.
The two-point score
The headline change is long-range scoring. A kick from outside the 40-metre arc is now worth two points rather than one. A new arc, starting on the 20m line and extending 40m from goal, is marked on every pitch so players and officials know where the boundary sits.
There was one wrinkle during the trial season. Under the 2025 rules, a score from outside the arc was still only counted as a single point if an opponent got a touch on the ball as it travelled over the bar. That condition caused a fair amount of controversy and has since been removed. For 2026, all scores from outside the arc are now rewarded with two points, which makes the market far cleaner to read.
How this changes the scoring shorthand
It is worth updating the way you read a scoreline. A goal is still worth three points, but the "points" tally now blends one-point and two-point scores, so you can no longer work out the breakdown from the scoreline alone. This is why scores are now usually shown with a combined total in brackets — for example, 4-18 (30) — and that bracketed figure is the one to rely on for a team's overall total. Scores can climb more quickly than they used to, so historic averages from before 2025 are not a reliable guide to modern totals.
Other rules worth knowing
A few of the other enhancements shape the flow of a game and are useful context when weighing up a match:
The 3v3 rule requires three outfield players from each team to be in each half at all times, which opens the pitch up and creates more attacking space. All kick-outs must travel beyond the 20-metre line, removing the short restart. The solo-and-go allows a player to play on immediately when awarded a free, speeding the game up, and a new advanced mark operates inside the 20m line. There is also a stronger clampdown on dissent and cynical play, with frees advanced significantly up the pitch for infractions.
What it means for your bets
The practical upshot is that Gaelic Football is now a higher-scoring, faster spectacle. Long-range shooting is rewarded, defences have less cover, and totals have risen across the board. When assessing Total Points and handicap markets, lean on data from the 2025 season onwards rather than older seasons, and pay attention to which teams have genuine two-point threats from distance, as that ability can swing a handicap line on its own.

FAQ
Gaelic Football Betting FAQs
What is a two-point score in Gaelic Football?
It is a score awarded for a successful kick taken from beyond the 40-metre arc, and it counts as double an ordinary point. The arc is marked on every pitch as a guide, and any converted attempt from outside it now earns the two points. Introduced as part of the sport's recent rule overhaul, it rewards long-range shooting and has pushed match totals higher, so it is well worth factoring in when weighing up a Total Points or handicap bet.
How does scoring work in Gaelic Football?
Three scoring values apply: three for a goal, one for a point from inside the 40-metre arc, and two for a point from outside it. Because the middle figure in a scoreline now blends one-point and two-point efforts, the total shown in brackets is the reliable guide to how many a team actually managed. Take 4-18 (30) as an example: the 30 is the true total, made up of four goals and eighteen points' worth of scores from range and close in.
What are the main Gaelic Football competitions to bet on?
Spring is dominated by the Allianz National Football League, before attention turns to the summer's inter-county action in the provincial championships, the Tailteann Cup and the All-Ireland race for the Sam Maguire Cup. Beyond the county game, there is plenty to follow in club football, the Sigerson Cup at third level, and the Ladies' game.
What betting markets are available for Gaelic Football?
The staple is the Full-Time Result, backing a home win, an away win or the draw. Alongside it, you will often find Total Points, an over/under call on the combined score, and the 3-Way Handicap, which hands one side a points start while still allowing the draw as a separate outcome.
Which team has won the most All-Ireland Football Championships?
Kerry sit top of the all-time list on 39 titles, with Dublin next on 31. No other county has reached double figures, which leaves those two some way clear of the chasing pack.
Can you bet on club Gaelic Football?
You can. The club game runs through county and provincial stages up to an All-Ireland final, with the showpiece traditionally landing on St Patrick's Day. It provides a steady stream of fixtures well beyond the inter-county calendar.
