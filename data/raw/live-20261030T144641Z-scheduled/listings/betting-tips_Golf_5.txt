Golf Betting Markets We Cover
Under the "Tournaments" tab, you will find predictions for a range of golf betting markets, including:
Tournament Outright Winner
This prediction is for a golfer to win the tournament. It can be a win bet, where the golfer has to win the event, or an each way selection, where the golfer usually needs to finish in the top five to eight places for a partial payout. Place terms vary from tournament to tournament and are often a quarter or a fifth of the odds for the place part of the bet. Bookmakers may offer up to eight or even ten places for big tournaments such as the Masters and the US Open. Place terms are displayed alongside the odds before you place a bet, so check you are happy with them before you finalise any wager.
First Round Leader
This is similar to the outright winner market, but the tip is for the golfer leading after round one. Selections can again be placed to win or each way. Each way terms usually cover only the first few places, and the number of places varies by bookmaker, so check before placing a bet.
Top American, Top European and Top GB & Ireland
These markets focus on golfers from a single region. You are still betting on which golfer finishes in the best position, but they only need to beat the other players in that market, not win the tournament. Each way betting is usually available in these markets for the main events, but not always for regular weekly tournaments, depending on how many players from the region are in the field.
Team Event Markets
For team events such as the Ryder Cup and Presidents Cup, we also cover three markets:
Overall correct score: the final points score between the two teams.
Top combined points scorer: the player who scores the most points across both teams.
Top team points scorer: the player who scores the most points for their own team.

The selection odds are shown alongside the number of tips received compared with the total number of tips on all outcomes in the market, which gives the confidence percentage. Value Rating
The Value Rating column shows a rating of one to five stars, with five stars indicating the strongest value. It compares the percentage of tips a selection has received with the chance of winning implied by its odds. If the tip percentage is higher than the chance implied by the odds, that is a sign the selection could be a value bet. Click the Value Rating column to sort selections by number of stars.

Golf Betting Tips: Course History and Previous Course Form
As in most sports betting, there are many factors to weigh up before placing a golf bet, and a player's previous course history is one of them. This section looks at it in more detail.
The phrase 'horses for courses' applies in golf too: some golfers play well on particular courses regardless of their current form. Combine course history with current form when weighing up golf betting tips and predictions for each event.
Is Good Previous Course Form Coincidental?
It is unlikely to be coincidence. A particular course set-up may suit a player's strengths, making them a good 'course fit'. Other courses with a similar layout, which demand the same game strategy, may also bring consistent results.
Here are a few reasons why a player may produce good results at a particular course, and at others with a similar layout and set-up.
Forgiving courses: wide fairways and light rough suit the players who hit the ball furthest off the tee, because distance matters more than accuracy.
Driving accuracy: the reverse of the above. Courses that reward accuracy off the tee favour players who rank highly for driving accuracy.
Putting greens: two types of grass, Bermuda and bentgrass, are commonly used on greens, and a player may favour one over the other. Bentgrass is mainly found on courses in the northern United States, while Bermuda suits the warmer climate of the South.
Over time, a golfer's course form can change for better or worse. Common reasons include a change in general form, an improvement or decline in their game, or a shift in focus from distance to accuracy off the tee.
The [href=/betting-tips/Golf/All_Golf/All_Events/US_Masters/5] US Masters is a good example of an event where a player's record can be analysed from a reliable dataset, because it is always held at Augusta National. A top-ten finish is never guaranteed, but the numbers highlight the golfers who play well there year after year, and those who struggle despite performing well on tour generally. For more on past results at Augusta, see our [href=/blogs/us-masters-betting-tips-guide] US Masters betting tips guide.
Tournaments occasionally change venue, so check whether past results came at the same course. Previous tournament form can give a different picture from previous course form.

In-Form Golfers: Using Recent Results for Golf Betting Tips
Recent tournament results are a quick way to build a shortlist of in-form golfers to consider when placing golf bets. Players heading into an event on the back of several good performances are a solid starting point when compiling golf picks and predictions.
Form: a tournament win, or several top-10 finishes in the last few events, shows a golfer's game is in a good place.
Mentality: winning and playing consistently well builds confidence, which can improve the chances of more success.
Settled preparation: golfers in form are far less likely to tinker with their swing or putting, try new clubs or change coach before the next event, any of which can create uncertainty for punters. Tinkering tends to follow a long spell of poor form.

Pitfalls of Backing In-Form Golfers
There are pitfalls to consider before blindly backing golfers in form, for example:
Value odds: a player in a rich vein of form will usually be priced accordingly for their next event. Do the odds fairly reflect their chance?
Other influences: keep up to date with any news that may affect performance since their last event, such as health, fitness or off-course issues.
Previous course form: a player may be in excellent form, but if their game does not suit a particular course, will it be any different this year? See our [href=#golf-betting-tips-course-history-and-previous-course-form] course history section for more.
World Golf Rankings: The OWGR Top 15
Who is the best golfer in the world is a question that divides golf fans and punters. Some point to the player who wins most often; others to a golfer's ability at the highest level in events such as the [href=/betting-tips/Golf/All_Golf/All_Events/Open_Championship/5] Open Championship. 'Best' also means best right now, which is a different question from who is the greatest golfer of all time.
The Official World Golf Ranking (OWGR) is a recognised system for rating professional male golfers. Players earn points for their finishing positions in eligible tournaments over a rolling two-year period, with more recent results carrying greater weight.
Official World Golf Ranking: Top 15
The OWGR is also used to decide player eligibility for the major championships and the Olympics. The table below shows the current top 15 male golfers, ranked by average points.
A high world ranking is a useful starting point for golf betting tips and predictions, but it is not a tip in itself. The leading players are usually short-priced, so look at current form, course history and value in the odds before deciding on a selection.

What does the Value Rating mean on golf tips?
The Value Rating shows a rating of one to five stars, with five stars indicating the strongest value. It compares the percentage of tips a selection has received with the chance of winning implied by its odds. If the tip percentage is higher than the chance implied by the odds, the selection could be a value bet. Click the Value Rating column to sort selections by stars. For more on the tips table, see [href=#free-golf-betting-tips-and-predictions-how-they-work] how our golf tips work.
Who is the best golfer in the world right now?
The Official World Golf Ranking (OWGR) is a recognised way to rate professional male golfers, based on their results over a rolling two-year period. The [href=#world-golf-rankings-the-owgr-top-15] current world top 15 is shown on this page and updates automatically, so check it for the latest ranking.
How do I check a tipster’s record before following a golf tip?
Each tip shows the tipster's level stakes profit, strike rate and other stats we highlight, such as a current hot streak, along with their analysis where they have added it. To compare tipsters' records across golf, see our [href=/best-tipsters/Golf/5] best golf tipsters page.
