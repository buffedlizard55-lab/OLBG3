Method, model and sources

Three layers: what OLBG published, what an official source confirmed, and what our model produced. They are kept apart on purpose so you can audit any single number.

1 · Our line model

For each fixture where OLBG's most-tipped market is a winner market we take the tip split, shrink it with a Beta(2,2) prior of strength four, then apply a sport-specific home-field shift in logit space. Small samples are pulled toward even money, so a 1/1 split becomes 60% rather than 100%. Prices are 1 / (p × 1.05).

Handicaps come from −Φ⁻¹(p) × σsport rounded to the nearest half point, where σ is our assumed margin dispersion (basketball 11.5 points, ice hockey 1.8 goals, snooker 2.3 frames, and so on). Totals are anchored to a per-sport baseline, not to per-match scoring data — we could not verify pace, weather or injuries fixture by fixture.

These are our own lines, as requested. They are not bookmaker prices and no bookmaker price is copied. The full parameter tables are in data/model.md in the repository, and tools/originate.py reproduces every number.

Confidence ladder

  • High — the fixture has a written prediction backed by official/primary sources for form and results.
  • Medium — winner market, at least five tips, and our probability is at least 0.08 from even money.
  • Low — small samples, rows where OLBG's market was not the winner market, or outrights with no field size.

2 · Verification rule

A value is written only when the linked source states it. If a source is silent, the field is blank and the row is flagged. Competition names are the clearest example: OLBG's rugby union, volleyball, handball and tennis listings do not name the competition, so 36 of 137 rows show "not stated" instead of a guessed league.
