# session-3271b876 — the clarity pass: one list of written picks, and where it sits

Branch: `arena/3271b876-olbg3`. Claim logged in `COORDINATION.md`. Base: `d0a765c` (`main`, after PR #167).

The complaint this session answers: **"the sites are too confusing to understand what the matches are and
where the picks are … we are only interested in fully written unique predictions, not just picking who will
win without any analysis."** So the deliverable is not more rows — it is one surface that answers *what are
the picks, what did you pick, and where is the analysis*, and a rule that a bare winner is never counted as
a pick.

## 1. The surface

`scripts/pick_surface.py` (new, this session) merges the two boards on the **OLBG event id** — the only key
both tracks agree on, because the root and `/olbg/` tracks number the same fixture differently (`VOL-29` vs
`VOL-36996`, and the FL-199 class of `BSB-160792`/`BSB-160789` mismatches). One card per fixture, per sport,
grouped by sport, with:

* both boards' selections shown side by side when the two tracks disagree (labelled, never averaged);
* the price we originate, decimal and American;
* our probability for that selection;
* the confidence pill;
* **the full write-up inline** — the prose is on the card, not one click away behind a "read the prediction"
  link;
* a source link per quoted claim, plus the OLBG event page for the row;
* `n / 5` for the sport, so a sport that is short says so in its own heading.

**Admission rule: a fixture appears as a pick only if it carries ≥30 words of written analysis on at least
one board.** A fixture with a selection but no prose is demoted and its mechanical reason is printed
("no written prediction for this fixture"), so a reader can tell *nothing written* from *nothing selected*.
This is the standing brief's rule turned into a filter in the builder rather than a promise in prose.

At this build: **17 pick cards, 24 written pieces, 0 held back** (Ice Hockey 5, Handball 5, Volleyball 3,
Rugby Union 1, Baseball 1, Gaelic Football 1, Motor Racing 1; 7 cards carry write-ups from both boards;
prose runs 60–147 words). Five sports have nothing written at all and the pages say so rather than padding
the list.
